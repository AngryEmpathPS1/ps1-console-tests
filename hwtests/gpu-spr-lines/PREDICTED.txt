HOW THE CORE GETS THERE. A 4- / 8-bit sprite's time is its rows' law plus FillWB's cycles (bare: gpu-spr-hold's blanking
column, 8b 256 wide ~457 a row, 128 wide ~230, 4b 256 wide ~294), and scanPos adds every line event the drawing meets
where it falls - the line hold, the scan-out's first fetch and its refills - each whole less a fixed absorb. A step is
the bare row plus the events a row's time meets (8b 256 wide: 483 of 2,152.5 a line); one well over it where the
drawing's END catches an event whole (535, 548) and the next under it (452) as that event moves inside the drawing and
is absorbed. Nothing here lets rows BEHIND an event take any of it back: the mean step does not fall with the height. E and D320 are the
same numbers here (the empty range leaves this core's scan-out running, as gpu-tex-noscan's consoles read alike).

WHAT THE CONSOLES DECIDE.
- Steps UNDER the bare row after an event (the rows behind it taking some of it back): the drawing catches up, and a
  long sprite pays less for each event than a short one - gpu-spr-hold's two sprites near a line long, and the
  256x256's 13 cycles a line.
- The same steps as here but each a cycle or so lower on the 256-wide families: the row law's own error.
- The events' sizes themselves (a step less the bare row) against this core's: the absorb.
- E against D320 on the consoles, to the cycle.
