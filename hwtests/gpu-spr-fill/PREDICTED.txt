WHAT EACH ANSWER WOULD MEAN (16 rows a sprite; f = a fill that is not a row's first, g = the first texel's):
- 4 drawn B: f = (u48 w80 - u0 w80) / 48, g = (u64 w64 - u0 w64) / 16 - 3 f. Here f is 9.77 cycles and g the same on
  v0.377.342; with the proposal f 10.02 and g 9.00 (1.02 less). A console with g about a cycle under f: the first fill, as
  proposed. g equal to f, and u0 w80 (ONE fill a row, its last) a cycle a row under what f makes it: "a row with a fill" - the
  count in land_trl.js changes from fills at the first texel to rows with a fill. Neither: the row term is something else, and
  the A rows (W 128 / 256 / 320) say what it goes with.
- u64 w80 is the check: 5 fills, the first among them = u64 w64 + one f + 8 texel pairs.
- the 8-bit B rows the same with 8 lines a row: f = (u56 w72 - u0 w72) / 128.
- clear rows: the same differences with nothing written. This core charges a clear fill what a drawn one costs less its
  write turnaround (2.23 ticks, 1.41 cycles, a fill) and a clear texel pair one cycle. A console's clear f and its clear W 64
  row (no fills: the texel pairs alone, 544 here against the drawn 569) are the two numbers gpu/bandwidth's row is made of.
- C: this core reads the modulated and the semi clear rows as the raw one to a few cycles (4,231-4,253). gpu/bandwidth's
  console read its semi twin 1 hblank in 15,138 off the plain one; here they differ 136 hblanks on the display - if the C
  rows agree on the console as they do here, that difference is the display's (the semi sprite's class of scan-out toll
  charged to a sprite that reads nothing back), not the blanking's.
- THE CACHE ITSELF: every fill count above is this core's cache model (four lines of 16 texels a row at 4-bit, eight of 8 at
  8-bit, line = the 4-word group's number & 3 / & 7). A row that reads ~10 cycles x 16 off its neighbours' pattern says the
  cache is not that.
Nothing is modelled from one run: six runs on each console first (rule 3).
